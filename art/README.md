# art/ — procedural Blender art source

Every custom model in `game/assets/custom/` is generated by a script here.
The `.glb` files and baked textures are committed, so **the game never needs
Blender**. Blender is only needed to change or rebuild art.

## Setup and build

```bash
tools/setup_blender.sh                 # installs bpy 4.5 into .venv-blender/ (git-ignored)
tools/build_art.sh                     # rebuild everything (Cycles CPU bakes; slow)
tools/build_art.sh characters/leo      # rebuild one asset
```

Previews (Cycles renders) go to `build/art_previews/`, or to
`$ART_PREVIEW_DIR` if set. Review them before importing.

## Conventions

- **Colours** in scripts are sRGB; `common.srgb()` converts them.
- **Material names** ending in `_painted` get Godot's painted style
  (`scripts/utilities/stylize.gd`). The hero and shadow profiles also add an
  ink outline.
- **Tints:** `style_tint` metadata (`Stylize.TINT_META`) on a mesh or a
  parent multiplies its albedo, painted materials included. The badge
  cosmetics use it (`scripts/meta/cosmetics.gd`): a hero's outfit mesh
  (`HeroDefinition.outfit_mesh`) and the tent. Keep tints light, since
  they colour the whole texture.
- **Emissive** flat materials (eyes, wand star, embers) render unshaded.
- **Triangle budgets:** imps ≤ 12k; the hero kids ≤ 40k (about 35k: body and clothes ~18–20k, head and ears ~4.5k, hair ~9–10k, each prop ~1.5k; Godot makes distance LODs on import), trees ≤ 5.2k (M8: about 4k of it is scattered leaves; pines 3.6k, berry bush 4.5k), props ≤ 1.5k. The kids bake at 1024 (they are under 100 px on screen) and export WebP textures.
- **High-to-low bake:** the kid scripts build dense meshes (voxel cloth with folds, the 100-ring head loft, per-vertex colours) and `chibi.lowpoly(mesh, tris)` decimates each one to its budget while keeping a dense copy. `paint_bake.paint(mesh, high=copy)` paints the look on the dense copy (so stripes, folds, AO and the face stay crisp), then transfers it onto the low mesh with a selected-to-active colour bake. `HIGH_POLY=1` skips the decimation, to compare.
- **Prop detail:** model props dense and finish them with `detail.bake_prop(parts, name, budget, ray=...)`, so carved detail (bark, plank seams, tiles, stitching) lives in the texture. Check a model in the game look with `game/tools/model_gallery.tscn` (usage is in its header).
- **Draft bakes:** `BAKE_SIZE=512 .venv-blender/bin/python art/characters/nela.py` bakes the painted look at low resolution with fewer samples (about a minute per kid at 256, a few minutes at 512–1024) and renders the previews, but never exports to `game/assets` (unless `DRAFT_EXPORT=1`, for a quick look in Godot; do not commit those). Use it to review paint between milestones; run the full 1024 bake only for a release.
- **Clip review:** `.venv-blender/bin/python art/review/clip_sheet.py leo walk`
  renders one kid clip (a state from the hero data, or a clip name) from
  the committed GLB in matte clay, from the gameplay camera's pitch and
  lens: a strip of 8 frames and a GIF at the game's play speed, in
  `build/clip_sheets/`. Walk and run travel over a checked ground at
  the game's speed, so sliding feet show. Options (`--azimuth`,
  `--frames`, `--travel`, `--size`) are in its docstring. The build
  states (`hammer`, `tinker`) hold the mallet, as the game does. Rebuild the
  kid first to review a new clip. The game's numbers it needs (the hero
  data, play speeds, camera, walk speed, mallet scale) come from
  `art/review/game_numbers.json`, which the game's validator checks
  against the game; after changing one of them, write it again with
  `godot --headless --path game res://tools/validate_project.tscn -- --write-clip-review`. In the game, `game/tools/hero_capture.tscn`
  records the same states as the player and the companion, and the
  validator measures slide, loop pops and action lengths
  (`docs/testing/test-strategy.md`, "Hero clip checks").
- **Sculpt review:** `SCULPT=1 QUICK=1 .venv-blender/bin/python art/characters/leo.py` renders matte clay views (front, 3/4, side, back), game-size silhouettes and clay animation frames, without baking (`preview.render_clay`, `preview.render_silhouette`).
- **Shape kit (chibi):** `limb` (elliptical lofted sections for anatomy), `fold` (sculpted cloth folds: parallel or radiating), `hem_ring` (rolled hems), `sculpted_hand`, `sculpted_boot`, `webbing`, `buckle`, `bedroll_detail`, `hair_clump(sharp=, twist=)`.
- **Patterned cloth** (stripes, folk patterns) is voxel-remeshed without decimation, so painted bands stay crisp.
- **Painted wear:** `common.grime(obj, colour, amount_fn)` blends dirt, scuffs and mud over the colours already set, before the bake.
- **Rigid gear** on characters is bound to one bone at weight 1.0.
  Weapons are built in the `handslot.r` bone frame: handle along +Y, blade
  toward -X.
- **Spring bones** (hero animation): whatever hangs or sticks out on a
  kid swings on a short chain of bones instead of being bound rigidly.
  A builder lists its chains (`CHAINS` in `leo.py`: name -> parent bone
  and 3 joints in rest space before the stretch) and calls
  `chibi.add_chain(rig, name, joints, parent, prop=PROP)` after
  `chibi.finish`, so the auto-weighted clothes never pick the bones up.
  That adds `<name>.1`, `<name>.2` (deforming) and a non-deforming
  `<name>.end` at the tip. `chibi.weight_along(obj, points, anchor, bones)`
  weights a part along a polyline, from the anchor at the root to the
  last bone at the tip: a hair lock along its own centre line (Leo's
  `clump(..., chain=)`), a bag along the chain's joints (pass such a
  rigid piece to `finish` with bone `None`, so it keeps its weights).
  No clip keys the chain bones. In the game, the hero data's
  `spring_chains` (`SpringChain`: the bones, stiffness, damping, gravity
  and a reach limit in degrees) tell `CharacterVisual` which to swing
  with `SpringChainModifier`. Leo has five: `hair.front` (the fringe),
  `hair.back` (the nape tufts), `hair.l` / `hair.r` (the side tufts) and
  `pack` (the bag hangs from its top; the harness, sternum strap and
  compass stay on the chest). Nela has eight: `hair.front` (the fringe
  wisps), `hair.back` (the nape locks), `hair.l` / `hair.r` (the
  face-framing locks), `hair.bun` (one bone: the whole top bun wobbles),
  `pack` (her bunny rides on `pack.1`), `scarf` (parented to `pack.1`,
  so it hangs off the swinging bag) and `lantern` (one bone from
  `hand.r`, hung along the hand slot after the stretch; the lantern and
  its glow are bound to it whole). Mum has four, at no extra triangles:
  `hair.l` / `hair.r` (the long side fall and the face-framing locks),
  `hair.back` (the hair under her bun) and `pack` (with the mug; her
  head-space joints go through `to_adult()` like her hair). Her bun and
  crown stay on the head. Mum's tuning is `MumArrival.SPRINGS` (she has
  no hero data). The validator checks the chains against the model, for
  Mum too.
- **Face atlas** (hero animation): the kids blink and change
  expression without bones. `face_paint.expression(FACE, state)` gives
  the face in each of `face_paint.STATES`: `open` (the neutral face),
  `closed` (the blink), `happy` (smiling arcs and an open grin, for a
  cheer), `hurt` (screwed-shut `> <` eyes, knitted brows, a gritted
  frown, for a hit) and `dazed` (spiral eyes, one brow up, a wobbly
  mouth, while down). In place of the head's own `paint_bake.paint`
  call, a builder calls `face_atlas.paint_head(head, high, FACE,
  params, kid)` (`art/characters/face_atlas.py`). It paints every
  state, bakes each under the head's own painted lighting onto the
  front-projected face plane (so a cell matches the baked head around
  it), and keeps one window from the brows to below the mouth. Then it
  paints the head as before, gives the head's front faces inside the
  window their own material (`<Kid>_Face_painted`, the same texture)
  with the face-plane UVs as the second UV map, and writes
  `game/assets/custom/<kid>_face.webp` (the cells, about 670×780 for a
  kid, one small texture beside the 1024 bakes) and `<kid>_face.tres`
  (a `FaceAtlas`: the window, the cells and the states). The hero data
  points at it (`face_atlas`); in the game `CharacterVisual` swaps the
  face surface to `painted_face.gdshader` and shows one cell at a time.
  The atlas adds no triangles. Mum has only `open` and `closed`
  (`face_atlas.paint_head(..., states=("open", "closed"))`), so she
  blinks. The validator checks each atlas against its model, its states
  and a 1024 texture limit. To review the paint without a build, paint
  the states with `face_paint.paint_face(face_paint.expression(...))`;
  `hero_capture.tscn ... --face` records the face close up in the game.

## Audio

All sound is procedural too: numpy synthesis in `art/audio/`, written as
`.ogg` to `game/assets/audio/` (committed, so neither the game nor Blender
needs Python). `tools/build_audio.sh` creates `.venv-audio/` (numpy and
soundfile) on first run.

| Path | What |
|------|------|
| `audio/dsp.py` | Oscillators, envelopes, filters, a convolution reverb, and instruments: `pluck` (Karplus-Strong guitar), `bell`, `pad`, `knock` (wood), `owl`. `write()` normalises and encodes the result |
| `audio/sfx.py` | One-shots in the `SOUNDS` table. Each id is written as `sfx/<id>_<n>.ogg` (variants). Ids match `Fx.burst` kinds where one exists. `chirp_leo` / `chirp_nela` are the barks' babble until recordings arrive (feature 037) |
| `audio/music.py` | Seamless loops: `music/day.ogg`, `music/night.ogg`, and `ambience/{day,night,campfire}.ogg` |
| `audio/voice.py` | Generated voice lines: every story line and bark, spoken with Kokoro TTS (Apache-2.0, offline) and written as `voice/<line id>.ogg`. Casting is the `CAST` table; `--audition` renders candidates with their measured pitch. Run `tools/build_audio.sh voice [id prefix or speaker]`; it installs `kokoro-onnx` and downloads the model (~350 MB, gitignored) on first use. Placeholders until real recordings replace them |

- **Tone:** warm, toy-like and never harsh (the players are young kids). Imps are silly ("poof" and a squeak), not scary.
- **Adding a sound:**
  1. Write a function and add it to `SOUNDS`.
  2. Run `tools/build_audio.sh sfx <id>`.
  3. Add the id to `volumes` in `game/resources/audio/audio_library.tres`. `validate_project` fails otherwise, and also when an `Fx.burst` kind has no sound.
- **Loops** fold their tails back to the start, so they wrap without a seam. Music uses whole cycles; ambience uses a short crossfade.
