# Feature 038: Decisions

- **From the 2026-10 brainstorm.** The user chose story (B) and choices / meta (C), with an **adventure** tone (see `docs/design/story.md`), and asked for the roadmap first.
- **The boss is never killed, only calmed,** to fit the tone: the shadows are lost things, not evil.
- **Chapter 2's last night lasts until the Moon-Eater is calmed.** `TimeManager.hold_night` stops the clock at 0 while it stands.
  - Calming it ends the night: the spawner sees the wave is cleared and dawn comes, so "win on boss defeat" and "win at dawn" are one path.
  - The debug skip and Chapter 1 still win at dawn as before.
  - Why: dawn rescuing the kids from the final boss would undercut the true ending.
- **Phase 3, the reach.** Below 25% it walks to the Moon Lantern and drinks its light.
  - The kids get `6 + 3 × stage` seconds (a full lantern 24 s, half 15 s).
  - Their hits do half damage while it curls round the lantern, and the lantern burns it for 2 × stage every 1.5 s.
  - If the time runs out, the lantern loses 2 stages, the boss heals to 45% and goes back to phase 2.
  - With a dark lantern there is nothing to eat, so it fights on to the end.
  - So the lantern decides how hard the ending is, but a run is never unwinnable. On paper, a level 5 party wins the reach with a full lantern, and a half lantern is tight.
- **Numbers (tune by playing):**
  - 800 HP, speed 0.9, a 3 s cooldown;
  - stomp: 10 damage, a 3.2 m ring, 0.9 s windup;
  - sweep: 7 damage, 5 m and 140°, 0.8 s windup;
  - campfire 6 damage, buildings ×3;
  - summons: 3 imps every 16 s, capped at 14 mobs;
  - no loot, 120 XP.
- **Calming it clears the field.** The other shadows fade, and any escort still to come is cancelled: the moon is back.
- **The eyes are Chapter 2 only (nights 5–6).** There's no night-3 mini boss in Chapter 1; this answers the open question in the handoff. It could come later.
- **The stand-in is built in code** (like the 036 lantern) with tween clips, and is about 3.6 m tall (scale 0.72). At 5 m it towered off the screen.
- **The boss gets a HUD bar, not an overhead bar.** The rise banner says "The Moon-Eater rises!" so it doesn't just repeat the bar's title.
- **Barks:** two triggers, both urgent, so they get through the nightfall chatter.
- **It's a golem (2026-10-18).** The team's character sheet (`art/reference/concept-2026-10/15_moon_eater_character_sheet.webp`) made it a wood-and-stone golem, so the code stand-in was rebuilt after it.
  - The chest window is warm gold, as on the sheet, not moon-blue. The moonbeam when it lets the moon go stays moon-coloured.
  - The window tips up (`WINDOW_TILT`) and bulges out of the trunk, so it reads from the high game camera; the face tips up for the same reason.
  - The body leans forward from the hips (`LEAN`) for the sheet's ape-like hunch.
  - It gets the heroes' brown ink line, not the imps' purple one.
  - The ending keeps the story's beat (it shrinks and curls up by the fire), with the sheet's "shrink ending" folded in: planks and stones tumble off and its hoard floats out of its chest. (Superseded by the falling-apart ending below.)
- **A baked model with rigid parts (second pass, 2026-10-18).** The primitive golem read as a robot, so it was rebuilt in the Blender pipeline like the other creatures (dense model, painted high-to-low bake).
  - No skeleton: a golem is rigid, and the clips were already tweens on pivots. The GLB has one node per moving part with its origin at its pivot, so `MoonEaterVisual` swings `Head`, `ArmL` and `ArmR` directly, and nothing in `BossController` changed.
  - The glowing parts keep materials set in Godot, so the window can dim while it drains the lantern and everything warms in the shrink.
  - The lean, window and pivots are constants in both `moon_eater.py` and `moon_eater_visual.gd`; change them together.
- **The sheet's falling-apart ending (2026-10-18, decided with the user).** At 0 HP it sags, then falls apart: the golem hides and 18 loose pieces (baked with the model under a `Debris` node) drop into a heap where it stood. The crescent rises out of the heap into the moonbeam and its hoard floats up and fades. Then a small warm golem climbs out of the rubble and walks to the fire, as before. The heap stays until the scene ends (group `moon_eater_pile`).
  - The pieces are tweened, not physics: deterministic (fixed seed) and cheap, and the smoke test can check the heap.
- **The face after the sheet's close-ups.** A tall, slightly convex mask of upright planks with jagged chin ends, almond eye holes carved into the wood (the big one on the viewer's left), a glow just behind the plank faces plus a soft amber halo, and a hood of planks and moss over the top. The eyes sit near the surface because the decimated game mesh keeps only a shallow bowl.
